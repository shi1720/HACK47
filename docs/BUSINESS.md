# Batchlight business brief

**Status:** a working product and an unvalidated commercial hypothesis. No customer interviews, paid pilots, revenue, or measured customer savings are claimed. All forecasts and pricing below are assumptions for testing, not financial results.

## The first customer

Start with an owner-operated sauce or preserve maker that buys identifiable ingredient lots, produces repeat batches, and supplies a small number of retailers or wholesale buyers. The owner is both buyer and operator. This segment has a concrete traceability workflow that can be demonstrated without procurement integrations or a large implementation project.

The initial job is: “Given a suspect lot, show me the batches and recipients connected in my records, and show what I still need to investigate.” The recurring job is recording production well enough that the next rehearsal has fewer gaps. If users only open the product during an annual drill, a monthly subscription may be the wrong model.

## Why this product might win

Batchlight makes incomplete records visible as a first-class result. It follows material through intermediate batches and carries uncertainty into descendants. A maker can resolve a gap with new input evidence, update the live scope, and retain earlier uncertainty in a previously saved snapshot. The rehearsal produces a shareable evidence pack. Local use and queued offline changes suit a workspace where connectivity may be unreliable, subject to server reconciliation when a hosted account reconnects.

These are product choices, not a claim of unique intellectual property. Competitors can copy them. The potential advantage must come from low setup effort, repeated use, trustworthy records, relevant import adapters, and distribution through small-producer communities. None is an established moat today.

Batchlight should not try to replace purchasing, accounting, quality management, and production planning in its first release. It should become useful before a maker chooses to migrate all operations. The comparison to test is whether a maker makes fewer mistaken exclusions when explicit input uncertainty propagates into descendants, a correction requires new evidence, and earlier reports keep their original basis. Rehearsal, reports, and small-business pricing are already available elsewhere. We have not established that competitors lack equivalent correction workflows.

## Price and cost hypotheses

Proposed offer: free local rehearsal with synthetic data or a maker's own local records, and **US$29 per producer per month** for a hosted workspace. Payment collection is not implemented. The price, packaging, hosting allowances, and service commitments need validation.

The core trace engine has no per-query model fee. Application costs depend on storage, backup retention, traffic, and support. Do not describe hosted operation as free merely because a platform offers a trial.

Illustrative steady-state contribution per subscribed workspace:

| Monthly item | Assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Subscription revenue | $29.00 |
| Allocated infrastructure and backups | ($3.00) |
| Payment cost, assumed 3% + $0.30 | ($1.17) |
| Support, 10 minutes at $30/hour | ($5.00) |
| Contribution before acquisition, engineering, tax, and overhead | **$19.83** |
| Contribution margin under these assumptions | **68.4%** |

At 30 support minutes per month, contribution falls to $9.83, or 33.9%. At 60 minutes it becomes negative. This is why assisted onboarding, clear validation messages, and narrow scope matter commercially. These are scenario calculations, not quotes from a payment or hosting vendor. At this price, 100 paying producers imply $2,900 monthly recurring revenue before costs, churn, tax, and discounts. That is a milestone hypothesis, not a forecast.

## Validation plan

No outreach has been sent. Obtain consent before using real records, redact personal details, and agree on a retention period. Recruit through personal introductions, shared kitchens, maker markets, or producer groups. Use a neutral invitation: “I am testing a tool for tracing a supplier lot through batch and shipment records. Could I observe how you would handle a practice recall using records you are comfortable sharing?”

Run five initial sessions with makers matching the target segment. Ask about the last real traceability task before showing the product. Avoid leading with “Would you use this?”

1. “Walk me through how you recorded your most recent batch.”
2. “Where do ingredient lot references and customer shipment records live?”
3. “Has a supplier or buyer ever asked you to trace a lot? What happened?”
4. “Which records are hardest to complete when production is busy?”
5. “Show me what you would do if this ingredient lot needed investigation.”
6. “What do you currently pay in money or time to do this?”
7. After a hands-on rehearsal: “Which step would stop you using this next week?”
8. Offer a specific paid pilot at the proposed price and record the decision without pressure.

Observe rather than infer. With permission, time the existing workflow and the Batchlight workflow on comparable tasks. Record setup time separately. Count correct recipients, false exclusions, unresolved evidence, and operator corrections. A shorter task with wrong exclusions fails.

### Decision gates

- **Proceed:** at least three of five makers complete a rehearsal with their own records, at least two accept a paid pilot, and the workflow exposes no critical false exclusions.
- **Revise onboarding:** makers see value but cannot prepare their records without substantial help.
- **Revise pricing or model:** makers value occasional rehearsals but reject monthly payment. Test a service-assisted drill or annual plan before assuming demand.
- **Stop or change segment:** the problem is infrequent, existing software already solves it adequately, or record entry burden outweighs the benefit.

These are planned decision thresholds, not study results. A five-person pilot does not establish market demand.

## First 90 days after OFFGRID

Days 1-30: observe five rehearsals, document record gaps, and repair high-impact usability issues. Arrange qualified review of the uncertainty language and incident workflow.

Days 31-60: invite willing producers to a paid pilot after operational prerequisites are met. Monitor onboarding burden, recurring record entry, and support time. Add only import adapters that pilots repeatedly need.

Days 61-90: evaluate retention and repeat rehearsals. Seek one shared-kitchen or producer-network distribution partnership. Publish an anonymized case study only with consent and real measurements. If customers do not return, revise the product before expanding the feature set.

## Regulatory positioning

The FDA describes additional traceability requirements for covered foods and entities. Its current page distinguishes a proposed extension to 20 July 2028 from a Congressional instruction not to enforce before that date. Scope and exemptions vary. Batchlight makes no compliance certification claim and does not base its pitch on a January 2026 deadline. [FDA primary source](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods)

The business can stand on a practical job: improving the records used during a supplier investigation. Legal obligations and food-safety decisions require the producer's own qualified advice.
